4. Treat exact pane counts, unobserved rear elevations and individual hotel names as unresolved until a closer source or site map establishes them. The course-side terrace and tall grey main building are now confirmed by the supplied photographs below.

### Tall grey main building / western white-roof connector

The user identifies this as the main building. The photograph clearly shows **three full window storeys plus an exposed basement**, making **four visible window rows on the downhill course-facing side**. The earlier two-storey interpretation is incorrect. Its large height relative to adjoining red volumes is real. Absolute metres remain a lidar measurement, not a photo-derived number.

- Walls are light neutral grey, not the warm cream of the separate southeast block. Fine horizontal/vertical seams or panel joints are visible; neither coarse vertical timber battens nor a particular plaster system can be asserted at this resolution.
- The exposed basement/plinth is visibly darker grey and mottled, resembling stone or textured concrete. The lowest row is partly screened by rising turf toward the right; do not repeat full-height windows mechanically through the ground.
- Roof is pale silver/light grey, shallow pitched, with standing-seam lines and a thin darker ridge/eaves cap. Its footprint and ridges change direction at the kink. It is not terracotta tile and has no visible solar panel array in these supplied views.
- The course-facing elevation has a regular column rhythm on its two middle floors, mostly small paired windows with dark frames/glass. The top floor is **not a copy**: several central/right bays are substantially wider multi-pane glazed groups, alternating with smaller pairs. Basement windows are fewer/partially exposed.
- About seven facade bays can be followed across the visible broad face and kink, but exact bay-to-wall allocation and mullion counts should be traced on the image rather than treated as a surveyed count. The kink also foreshortens the right-hand bays.
- The left adjoining red lodging wing has two visible rows of white-trimmed windows and a red-brown tiled roof. Its ridge lies below the grey main building's upper windows. The right restaurant/clubhouse wing is lower, with a long tiled roof and dark continuous course-facing glazed veranda.
- The aerial confirms a long terrace/apron on the restaurant's course side, with repeated outdoor tables/chairs. It supports the long glazed/covered elevation, but not each opening's exact mullion count.

### Entrance detail from two close photographs

- The foyer is a projecting gable volume in front of burgundy red vertical-board walls. Its face and angled returns are charcoal irregular polygon stone with thin pale mortar lines, rather than regular masonry courses.
- Broad white/off-white bargeboards/fascia wrap both roof slopes and project beyond the stone face. The gable accommodates a large emblem and lettering above the doors.
- The portal has **four tall vertical glazed bays**: a fixed sidelight at each side and two central automatic sliding leaves. Frames and perimeter are sage/dark olive green; a small black motion sensor sits centred over the leaves. No large horizontal transom divides the glass.
- Two large black traditional faceted wall lanterns flank the doorway, one per side, on curved wall brackets.
- Signage is raised white lettering, `Ombergs Golf Resort`, casting a shadow. Above it is a separate three-colour emblem: yellow sun semicircle, slim curved blue wave and broad green hill/leaf shape.
- The entrance sits on a flat orange/red brick-paved rectangular apron adjoining dark asphalt. Large planters and small freestanding lanterns flank the doorway in the front view; these are movable dressing and not architectural dimensions.
